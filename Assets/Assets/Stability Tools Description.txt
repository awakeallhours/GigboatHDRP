Stability Tools Description

⚓ 1. StabilitySample
Purpose:
Represents one row of raw stability data collected at a specific heel angle.
What it contains:
- Heel angle (deg)
- GM at that angle
- GZ at that angle
Why it exists:
The scanner produces many samples — one per heel step.
This struct is the atomic unit of the stability dataset.
Analogy:
Think of it as a CSV row in a stability test log.

⚓ 2. StabilityProfile
Purpose:
A summary of the entire stability scan.
What it contains:
- GM at small heel (GM_initial)
- Maximum GM and the angle it occurs
- Maximum GZ and the angle it occurs
- Angle of vanishing stability (GZ crosses zero)
- COM safe band (min/max)
- Positive stability range
- Notes
Why it exists:
This is the final “report” you care about — the thing you can save, compare, export, or show in UI.
Analogy:
If StabilitySample is the raw data, StabilityProfile is the executive summary.

⚓ 3. StabilityProfileBuilder
Purpose:
Turns raw samples → final profile.
What it does:
- Sorts samples by heel
- Finds the first valid GM (initial GM)
- Finds the peak GM
- Finds the peak GZ
- Detects where GZ crosses zero (angle of vanishing stability)
- Computes positive stability range
- Packs everything into a StabilityProfile
Why it exists:
You don’t want analysis logic inside the scanner or the visualiser.
This keeps the pipeline clean and modular.
Analogy:
It’s the “post‑processing” step — like Unity’s lightmapper taking raw bounces and producing a final lightmap.

⚓ 4. GMGZStabilityScanner
Purpose:
Performs the actual physical scan in‑scene.
What it does:
- Rotates the boat from startAngle → endAngle
- Waits for buoyancy to settle at each step
- Computes heel, GM, and GZ
- Stores each result as a StabilitySample
- Passes the full list to StabilityProfileBuilder
- Returns a StabilityProfile via callback
Why it exists:
This is the runtime “experiment runner.”
It interacts with the real physics system, not just math.
Analogy:
It’s the machine that runs the test — like a tilt table in a shipyard.
