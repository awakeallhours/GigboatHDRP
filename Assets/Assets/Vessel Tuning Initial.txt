   🔵 1. Buoyancy Calibration (Your Buoyancy Calibrator Tool)
This is always Step 1 because it establishes the static equilibrium of the vessel.
What you do:
- Set target displacement
- Adjust mass until the boat floats at the correct draft
- Validate probe contributions
- Confirm waterplane area looks sane
- Ensure no probe is dominating or dead
What this gives you:
- Correct draft
- Correct displacement
- Correct waterplane
- Correct initial hydrostatics
This is the foundation of everything else.


🔵 2. GM/GZ Stability Scan (Your Stability Scanner)
This is Step 2 — before COM tuning — because GM/GZ tells you whether the hull itself is stable.
What you do:
- Heel the boat through 0–45°
- Capture GM and GZ at key angles
- Identify:
- GM_initial
- GM_peak
- GZ_peak
- Angle_of_max_GZ
- Positive_stability_range
What this gives you:
- The hull’s natural stability curve
- The hull’s righting behaviour
- The hull’s safe operating envelope
- A baseline to compare COM adjustments against
This step tells you what the hull wants to do.


🔵 3. COM Tuning (Vertical COM Adjustment)
Only after GM/GZ is validated do you tune COM.
Why?
Because COM tuning changes GM/GZ.
You need the baseline first.
What you do:
- Move COM up/down
- Watch GM_initial change
- Watch GZ_peak change
- Watch angle_of_max_GZ shift
- Find the “comfort zone” where:
- GM_initial is not too low
- GM_initial is not too high
- GZ_peak is healthy
- Roll behaviour feels correct
What this gives you:
- A safe COM band
- A recommended COM height
- A tuned roll feel
- A predictable stability profile
This is where you shape the boat’s personality.

🔵 4. Stability Band Creation (Diagnostic Output)
Now that GM/GZ and COM are tuned, you formalise the results.
What you record:
- GM_initial
- GM_peak
- GZ_peak
- Angle_of_max_GZ
- Angle_of_vanishing_stability (estimated)
- COM_safe_zone (min/max)
- Stability rating (optional)
What this gives you:
- A permanent stability profile
- A reference for future tuning
- A sanity check for future hull edits
- A user‑facing diagnostic for your framework
This is the “blueprint” of the vessel’s stability.


🔵 5. Roll Feel / Hydrodamping (Runtime Tuning)
This is the last step because it depends on:
- Correct buoyancy
- Correct GM/GZ
- Correct COM
What you tune:
- Roll damping
- Roll spring
- Added mass
- Viscous drag
- Wave‑induced roll response
What this gives you:
- The final “feel” of the vessel
- Smooth, believable roll
- No snappiness
- No sluggishness
This is where the boat becomes alive.
